step 1 Get on the radio to the casualty at once and establish two things from her master: whether every person aboard is accounted for and out of the fire space, and which space is actually burning. We have 1.9 hours before the fire reaches her fuel tanks and every decision below depends on that boundary.
step 2 Confirm the affected space is clear of personnel and that the boundary doors are shut, then have the master release the vessel's own fixed suppression into the space: gas flooding if this is the machinery space, foam if it is a cargo deck. A fixed system is the fastest water on the fire and it costs us nothing in ETA. It must not be activated until the muster confirms the space is empty.
step 3 Dispatch FFP-002 as the first firefighting unit (ETA 0.7 h) and put her 260 m3/h on boundary cooling from the outside: shell plating adjacent to the fire space and, above all, the bulkheads and decks between the fire and the fuel tanks. Buying time on that boundary is the whole object of the first hour.
step 4 Bring FFP-003 up next (ETA 1.2 h) and set her 240 m3/h on the second boundary, working the opposite side and the deck over the fire so that the space is being cooled from more than one face and the heat is not simply driven across to the tank boundary.
step 5 Put FFP-004 (ETA 1.2 h) on the fuel tank boundary itself as a dedicated 53 m3/h cooling stream. She is small, but the tank boundary is the thing that must not fail, and a modest stream held continuously on it is worth more than the same water used elsewhere.
step 6 Bring FFP-001 in as she arrives (ETA 1.7 h) and add her 230 m3/h to the attack, directed by whoever has eyes on the space at that point; she lands with only about 0.2 h in hand before the deadline, so her position must be assigned and her hoses laid out on passage, not worked out on arrival.
step 7 Fight the fire with all four units together: FFP-002 at 260 m3/h, FFP-003 at 240 m3/h, FFP-001 at 230 m3/h and FFP-004 at 53 m3/h deliver 783 m3/h against the 650 m3/h the architect requires, and every one of the four is on scene inside the 1.9 h window. Hold that combined delivery on the fire and on the tank boundary until the space is cooled and the fire is knocked down and declared out.
step 8 Use AST-001's three crew (ETA 1.6 h) as the on-water observation and safety party: they run the thermal readings on the shell plating so cooling is directed where the metal is actually hottest rather than where the smoke is thickest, keep watch for people in the water, and act as the communications link between the fire teams and the salvage master.
step 9 If what is burning is a water-soluble fuel or cargo, standard foam will be broken down by it and the attack must change to alcohol-resistant foam; establish this from the manifest in step 1 and tell the pump crews before they commit, because finding it out by watching the foam blanket collapse wastes water we do not have.
step 10 Do not ventilate or open a space that is superheated and smouldering. If the space is hot and dark rather than openly burning, cool it through the boundary from outside until the metal temperature has come down, then open it under a charged line; opening it hot invites a backdraft that will injure the attack team and set the fire back on us.
step 11 Once the fire is declared out, keep FFP-002 and FFP-003 on scene running a reflash watch with their monitors charged, and continue cooling the fuel tank boundary until thermal readings confirm the metal has come down and stays down.
step 12 With the fire out, have the hull and stability reassessed before anyone stands down. Fire fighting water accumulates high in the vessel and fire damage weakens structure; a vessel that has been saved from burning is at that moment exposed to capsizing or sinking from the water we put into her. Sound all compartments, establish where the firefighting water has gone, and dewater as the architect directs.
step 13 Assumptions and limitations: I have no photograph, so I do not know which space is involved, how far the fire has spread, or where the smoke is issuing from. Step 2's choice between gas flooding and foam, and the boundary assignments in steps 3 to 5, all depend on the master's report and FFP-002's first-on-scene observation and must be confirmed against them.
step 14 Limitation on resources: FFP-005 and her 280 m3/h cannot be counted. At ETA 3.0 h she arrives more than an hour after the fire is predicted to reach the fuel tanks, so she is no part of the 783 m3/h above. Order her forward anyway, because if the fire is not out at the deadline she is the only reinforcement available and she will be wanted for the reflash watch in any case.
step 15 Second limitation: this is tight. FFP-001 arrives at T+1.7 h against a T+1.9 h deadline, and without her 230 m3/h the three earlier units deliver only 553 m3/h, which is short of the required 650 m3/h. The plan therefore depends on FFP-001 making her ETA and being put to work immediately.
step 16 Requests: the cargo manifest and bunker tank plan, so I know whether this needs alcohol-resistant foam and exactly which boundary is the fuel tank boundary; confirmation of the crew muster before the fixed system is released; and a foam supply figure, since this ledger rates every unit in water delivery only and tells me nothing about how much foam concentrate is available if step 9 applies.
step 17 Contingency: if at the deadline the fire is still making ground on the tank boundary, stop trying to save the space and withdraw the attack teams to a safe distance, keep the monitors on the tank boundary from the longest reach available, and prepare for the possibility of a tank involvement rather than losing people to it.

step 1 Open the casualty on VHF and establish the salvage master's command net with the master of the grounded vessel; obtain her displacement, present drafts fore and aft, pre-grounding drafts, tank soundings and cargo manifest, and confirm whether anyone remains aboard in a compartment that is set down on the ground.
step 2 Dispatch TUG-003 (ETA 1.8 h) as first-on-scene unit; on arrival she establishes the exclusion zone, takes a full set of soundings around the hull with her echo sounder to map the ground under the vessel and the water depth on the escape heading, and sets a portable tide gauge on the casualty's side so ground reaction can be tracked against predicted high water rather than guessed at.
step 3 Have TUG-003's crew work through every tank and void with the vessel's own engineers, sounding for bunkers and confirming the hazardous-cargo position, before any load is put on the hull; nothing is pulled and no hot work is done until that round of soundings is signed off.
step 4 Order TUG-001 (ETA 2.4 h) and TUG-002 (ETA 3.4 h) to proceed at best speed and rig for a towing connection on passage so that they can be made fast within minutes of arrival rather than after it.
step 5 Bring AST-001 to the scene (ETA 2.7 h) and deploy its 600 m of containment boom as a close-in collar around the grounded hull, concentrated on the bilge and bunker-tank side and on the down-tide quarter, so that any oil released when the hull works free of the ground is held against the hull instead of running downstream; this boom is pollution containment only and contributes no pulling force.
step 6 While the tugs close, have the casualty's crew ballast down or pump out as directed by the naval architect to shift weight away from the neutral loading point and take strain off the bottom; any transfer must reduce ground reaction, not merely move water about, and the tide gauge readings are the test of whether it is working.
step 7 Make TUG-003 fast on the port quarter, TUG-001 fast on the starboard quarter, and TUG-002 on the centreline stern towing connection, all leading astern on the reciprocal of the grounding heading; use the casualty's own towing bitts and chain bridles, and set the leads so the three pulls are nearly parallel and genuinely add rather than fighting each other across an angle.
step 8 Take the strain progressively as the tide makes, starting around 60 per cent of rated pull about an hour before predicted high water, and hold station on the ground tackle while the rising water does the work of reducing ground reaction.
step 9 At high water, which falls at T+5.4 h, go to full combined pull with TUG-003, TUG-001 and TUG-002 together: 140 t plus 120 t plus 160 t gives 420 t of bollard pull against the 340 t the naval architect requires, and all three are on scene and connected inside the tidal window. Hold the full pull through the slack at the top of the tide and drag the vessel bodily astern off the shoal until she is afloat and answering her helm.
step 10 As soon as she comes free, have TUG-002 take and hold the tow to keep her off the shoal and under control while TUG-003 and TUG-001 slack down and come alongside to check that the hull has not opened; re-sound all tanks and bilges immediately, because a hull that has been pulled off ground frequently floods through plating that only failed on the way out.
step 11 With the casualty afloat and the tanks re-sounded, tow her under TUG-002 to the nominated sheltered anchorage for a diver and class inspection, keeping TUG-003 escorting; recover AST-001's boom only after the casualty is clear of the grounding site and the water around the shoal is confirmed clean.
step 12 Assumptions and limitations: I am planning without the photograph, so the vessel's actual list, the condition of her plating and whether she is lying bow-on or broadside to the shoal are unknown; those must be settled by TUG-003's first-on-scene report and the plan adjusted accordingly, particularly the lead angles in step 7.
step 13 Further limitation: BG-001 cannot be used for this refloat. Its 53 t of ground tackle would be welcome insurance against the pull coming up short, but at ETA 11.6 h the beach gear arrives more than six hours after high water and is therefore irrelevant to the tidal window; TUG-004 (ETA 15.2 h), TUG-005 (ETA 8.9 h) and TUG-006 (ETA 7.8 h) are likewise outside the window and are not counted in the 420 t.
step 14 Requests: confirmation of the predicted high-water height and time at the casualty, so that the 5.4 h figure can be tied to an actual tidal curve; the seabed type under the hull, since the friction coefficient against rock would raise the required pull well above the architect's 340 t figure and would argue for waiting for BG-001 and TUG-004 and pulling on the following tide instead; and a diver or ROV asset, which this ledger does not contain, to confirm there is no pinnacle or obstruction aft that the hull would be dragged over.
step 15 Contingency: if the pull at high water does not move her, do not work the tugs beyond rated pull or risk parting gear. Slack off, hold her with TUG-002, and plan a second attempt on the next high water with BG-001 laid as ground tackle astern and TUG-004 added, giving 629 t at T+15.2 h, with lightering into a barge requested in the interim if the architect's revised ground reaction demands it.

step 1 Fix the wreck's position and establish her condition before committing lift assets. Obtain the vessel's general arrangement, her lightship and loaded displacements, her bunker and tank plan and her cargo manifest from the owners, and get the depth of water over her and her attitude on the bottom from the reporting source. We have 47.3 hours until the weather window closes, which is enough time to do this properly, and it should be used that way.
step 2 Deploy AST-001 (ETA 4.7 h) and lay its 630 m of containment boom over the wreck position, set to the tidal stream and worked on the surface where oil is pooling. Oil from a sunken hull does not behave as one thing: establish by observation whether it is surfacing in a slick, suspended in the water column or settled on the bottom, and match the containment to that rather than booming the surface and declaring the problem solved.
step 3 Run a sonar search over and around the wreck to find oil that is not visible from the surface and to map the debris field and the wreck's exact attitude. Submerged and sunken oil will not show in the slick and will be missed entirely if the only survey is a visual one from a boat.
step 4 Bring LFT-004 up early (ETA 6.7 h) and use her as the on-site working platform and survey crane rather than as a principal lifting unit. Her 100 t is modest but she is the second asset available and she can set the moorings, place the rigging on the wreck and handle gear while the heavy cranes are still on passage.
step 5 Before any diver enters a compartment or any hot work begins, test each space in strict sequence: explosive gas first, then oxygen, then toxic gas. No space is entered on the strength of a single reading and no space is entered at all until all three tests are read and recorded.
step 6 Account for her fuel and her cargo before the hull is disturbed. Offload the bunkers by hot tapping or by whatever means the tank condition allows, and confirm the cargo position against the manifest. Lifting a hull with fuel still in her turns a wreck removal into a pollution incident the moment a tank is strained.
step 7 Patch and seal the openings in the hull as the survey identifies them, so that the hull is working with the cranes rather than against them, and so that whatever buoyancy she retains is not lost as she comes off the bottom.
step 8 Bring LFT-002 up as the principal crane (ETA 6.2 h) and position her over the wreck on a proper spread, then add LFT-003 (ETA 15.3 h) and LFT-001 (ETA 23.4 h) as they arrive, each moored independently so that none is relying on another to hold station under load.
step 9 Have divers pass the lifting slings or wires under the hull at the stations the naval architect nominates, with the leads set so that the four cranes share the load as the architect has calculated it and so that no single crane is taking more than its rating when the hull breaks the suction of the bottom.
step 10 Raise the wreck with all four cranes lifting together under one lift controller: LFT-002 at 460 t, LFT-003 at 390 t, LFT-001 at 380 t and LFT-004 at 100 t give 1330 t of lift capacity against the 1100 t the architect requires, and the last of them, LFT-001, is on scene at T+23.4 h, with more than twenty-three hours still inside the 47.3 h weather window. Take the initial break from the bottom slowly, because bottom suction will show as load well above the calculated submerged weight and then release suddenly.
step 11 Lift her clear of the bottom and bring her up in stages, holding at each stage for the load readings to settle and for the architect to confirm the rigging is behaving as calculated. As she approaches the surface, dewater her through the patches so that she is coming up on her own buoyancy as much as on the cranes, and watch for free surface, which on a hull that has been full of water is what will capsize her at the moment she becomes a floating object again.
step 12 Hold her in the cranes once she is on the surface and do not release. Pump her down to proper freeboard, have the architect confirm she has positive stability on her own lines, and only then slack the gear.
step 13 Bring AST-002 (ETA 31.6 h) alongside once she is up and use the barge to receive recovered cargo, bunkers, debris and anything lifted out of her; her 1600 t is deck capacity and no part of the lift calculation in step 10. She arrives inside the weather window, though late enough that nothing before step 11 may be made to depend on her.
step 14 Tow the raised hull clear of the site within the weather window, recover the lifting rigging and AST-001's boom last, and confirm by sonar and by diver that the site is clear of oil and debris before the operation is closed out.
step 15 Assumptions and limitations: I have no photograph of this casualty. The depth of water over her, her attitude on the bottom and the extent of her damage are therefore unknown to me, and they determine the method. I have planned a patch-and-lift because the architect has specified a lift capacity requirement, which implies the hull is to come up intact; if the survey finds her deeper than her gear can reach or too broken to raise in one piece, this becomes a sectional removal and the plan must be rewritten on that basis.
step 16 Second limitation: 1100 t against 1330 t available in the window is a margin of 230 t, or about seventeen per cent, and that margin is thin for a bottom-suction lift where the break-out load is genuinely uncertain. If the architect's figure moves up at all, this operation has a problem, because LFT-005 (ETA 65.6 h), LFT-007 (ETA 69.8 h) and LFT-006 (ETA 70.1 h) all arrive after the weather window has closed and cannot be substituted in. The 2070 t full-fleet figure is not available to this job.
step 17 Requests, and the first one is serious: this ledger contains no dive team and no ROV. Steps 5, 6, 7, 9 and 14 all require divers, and a sunken-vessel recovery cannot be rigged without them. I need a dive team assigned before the cranes arrive. I also need dewatering pumps, which this ledger does not contain and which step 11 depends on; sonar capability, which I have called for in step 3 and which is likewise not on the ledger; and the architect's break-out load allowance alongside his 1100 t submerged weight figure, since those are different numbers and only one of them is on the page.

# Gold plan notes

Written from `controls/gold_stimuli.txt` only. No photograph was available, so no plan
asserts a specific visual observation; where a real plan would turn on something only an
on-scene look could settle (fully inverted vs. on her beam ends, which space is burning,
hard vs. soft grounding) each plan states it as an assumption or a limitation and names the
asset whose first report resolves it.

Deadline rule applied throughout: an asset counts toward the requirement only if `ETA_H` is
at or inside the stated deadline. Capacity is summed from the per-asset figures, not read off
the "On scene within X h" line. Late assets are named explicitly and excluded, so a reviewer
can see they were considered rather than overlooked. The "Full fleet" line is treated as
unavailable whenever its gating asset lands after the deadline.

Scalar discipline: boom (m), barges (t deck), pumps (m3/h), dive teams (divers) and survey
launches (crew) are used for the work they can actually do and are stated, in the plan text,
to contribute nothing to the rated requirement. No plan sums across units.

- No dive or ROV asset on the ledger - requested, since nobody can confirm the escape track aft is clear.

## Ledger plausibility findings for the scenario generator

1. **Geography is incoherent, in every cell.** `Singapore anchorage` appears alongside Valletta, Gibraltar, Port Mahon, Algeciras, Piraeus, Las Palmas and Rotterdam, with ETAs that treat them as interchangeable. AGR-00017 has TUG-002 reaching the scene from Singapore anchorage in 3.4 h while BG-001 takes 11.6 h from Valletta. AGR-00018 has TUG-008 arriving from Singapore anchorage in 1.3 h and BG-002 taking 17.0 h from the same anchorage. ON_-00142 has Rotterdam at 0.5 h and Valletta at 0.7 h for the same casualty. ETA appears to be drawn independently of location, so location is noise. Two assets at the *same* named location with wildly different ETAs is the clearest tell.
2. **CAP-00076's fleet is not of this world.** Thirteen crane barges of identical 60000 t.m rating, ten reachable inside 12 hours, totalling 780000 t.m. Global heavy-lift availability is nothing like this; a real large-hull parbuckling is a multi-month mobilisation. The identical ratings also reduce the requirement to a pure division problem rather than a selection problem, which may weaken the cell as a test.
3. **Resource kits are incomplete in ways that matter more than the headline scalar.** Three of the four sunken/capsized cells have no dive team and no ROV (CAP-00019, CAP-00076, SUN-00064) although rigging a submerged or inverted hull is impossible without one. Both fire cells list no foam concentrate despite the domain text raising alcohol-resistant foam. Neither fire cell has a dewatering pump, though the same domain text warns that a fire-damaged vessel is then exposed to sinking. SUN-00027 has three cranes and one pump and literally no way to get rigging onto the wreck. A planner who only checks the rated scalar will score well while having written an unexecutable plan - worth knowing if the scoring logic only looks at the headline unit.
4. **Deadline and ETA coincidences look generated rather than designed.** SUN-00027's only in-window crane arrives at 31.7 h against a 31.8 h deadline (six minutes of slack); AGR-00018's TUG-006 arrives at exactly 3.3 h, the deadline itself. CAP-00019's mandatory RGT-002 lands 1.1 h before deadline, which is not enough time to rig a strand jack set in reality - so that cell is arithmetically sufficient but operationally marginal. If the intent is a clean SUFFICIENT / INFEASIBLE split, these boundary cases blur it.
5. **Distractor scalars are sometimes the only asset of their kind.** AGR-00017's BG-001 is the ledger's one piece of ground tackle *and* is out of window; AGR-00045 has no ground tackle at all. Fine as a test of scalar discipline, but it means the right maritime answer and the available answer diverge, which a plan has to argue rather than just execute.
6. **Non-matching-unit assets are consistently present and consistently useless**, which is good design: boom in metres, barges in tonnes of deck, pumps in m3/h, dive teams in divers, launches in crew. Each plan states explicitly that these contribute nothing to the rated requirement.

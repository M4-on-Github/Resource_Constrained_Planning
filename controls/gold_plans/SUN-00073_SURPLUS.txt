step 1 Establish the wreck's exact position and attitude and build the engineering case before anything is mobilised. Get her general arrangement, displacement, tank and bunker plan and cargo manifest from the owners, together with the depth of water over her. The weather window gives us 39.1 hours, which is time enough to survey properly, and a lift of this size that is rigged in a hurry is a lift that goes wrong.
step 2 Put AST-001's dive team on scene as the first asset (ETA 5.1 h) and have the five divers carry out the full wreck survey: her attitude on the bottom, how deeply she is embedded, the extent and position of her damage, the openings that will need patching, and the condition of the hull at every station the architect wants to pass a sling under.
step 3 Before any diver enters an enclosed space and before any hot work is done on her, test each space in sequence for explosive gas first, then for oxygen, then for toxic gas, and record all three. No space is entered on one reading and none is entered until the full sequence is read clear.
step 4 Have the dive team locate and account for her bunkers and her cargo against the manifest, and get the fuel off her before the hull is disturbed. A hull that is lifted with oil still in her will release it at the moment the tanks take strain, and at that point we have added a pollution response to a wreck removal.
step 5 Survey for released oil in the water as well as on the surface. Establish whether the oil is pooling on the surface, suspended in the column or settled on the bottom, and match the response to that behaviour; sunken and submerged oil will not appear in a surface slick and a sonar sweep is needed to find it.
step 6 Patch and seal the hull openings the divers have identified, so that she retains whatever buoyancy she has and so that the cranes are not fighting water flooding back in as she rises.
step 7 Use LFT-010 (ETA 6.4 h) and LFT-009 (ETA 8.9 h) as diver-handling and rigging aids rather than as lifting units. Their 10 t and 37 t of lift bag capacity is the right tool for lifting debris clear of the sling stations, recovering loose items, and taking the weight of heavy rigging while the divers work it into position; neither is counted toward the main lift.
step 8 Order LFT-008 (ETA 10.0 h), LFT-002 (ETA 11.4 h) and LFT-003 (ETA 12.6 h) forward as the principal lifting units and have them rig their gear on passage. Moor each on her own independent spread over the wreck as she arrives, positioned to the architect's lift plan so that no crane depends on another to hold station under load.
step 9 Have the divers pass the lifting wires under the hull at the architect's nominated stations and set the leads so that the three cranes share the load as calculated, with no crane taken above its rating at the moment the hull breaks the bottom.
step 10 Raise the wreck with the three cranes lifting together under one lift controller: LFT-008 at 520 t, LFT-002 at 600 t and LFT-003 at 520 t give 1640 t of lift capacity against the 1500 t the architect requires, and the last of them is on scene at T+12.6 h, leaving more than twenty-six hours of the 39.1 h weather window in hand. Break her off the bottom slowly, because suction will show as load well above her calculated submerged weight and will then let go all at once.
step 11 Hold LFT-005 (ETA 14.4 h) and LFT-006 (ETA 15.8 h) at the scene as a rigged reserve, a further 1140 t available well inside the window. I want them because the 1500 t figure is a pre-survey estimate, because bottom suction is the least predictable load in this trade, and because a crane that goes off-hire mid-lift with a hull hanging in the gear is otherwise a disaster. LFT-007 (26.3 h), LFT-001 (30.5 h) and LFT-004 (33.0 h) remain inside the window as a deeper reserve but are not mobilised unless the survey shows the lift to be substantially heavier than estimated.
step 12 Bring her up in stages, holding at each stage for the load readings to settle and for the architect to confirm the rigging is behaving as designed. Dewater through the patches as she nears the surface so she is coming up on her own buoyancy as well as on the wires, and watch the free surface in her; a hull that has been full of water has almost no stability at the moment she becomes a floating object again, and that is when she will roll.
step 13 Keep her hanging in LFT-008, LFT-002 and LFT-003 once she is on the surface. Pump her down to proper freeboard, have the architect confirm positive stability on her own lines, and only then slack the gear in sequence.
step 14 Recover the lifting rigging with the dive team from AST-001, then have the divers and a sonar sweep confirm the site is clear of oil, gear and debris before the operation is closed out, and tow the raised hull clear of the site inside the weather window.
step 15 Assumptions and limitations: I have no photograph, so her depth, her attitude on the bottom and the extent of her damage are unknown to me. I have planned an intact patch-and-lift recovery because the architect has specified a lift capacity, which implies the hull is to come up in one piece. If AST-001's survey finds her too deep for the cranes' reach or too badly broken to raise whole, this becomes a cut-and-lift sectional removal and the plan must be rebuilt from the survey, with the cranes taking her out in pieces onto a barge.
step 16 Limitation on resources: LFT-014 (ETA 39.7 h), LFT-012 (ETA 51.0 h), LFT-011 (ETA 53.9 h) and LFT-013 (ETA 66.9 h) all arrive at or after the close of the weather window and contribute nothing. The 5677 t full-fleet figure is not available to this operation; 4507 t is on scene inside the window, of which I have committed 1640 t and hold 1140 t rigged in reserve.
step 17 Requests: dewatering pumps, which this ledger does not contain and which steps 12 and 13 both require, and whose absence is the most serious gap in what I have been given; a barge, likewise absent, to receive recovered bunkers, cargo and debris and to take sections if step 15's contingency comes into effect; sonar capability for steps 5 and 14; and the architect's break-out load allowance stated separately from his 1500 t submerged weight, because those are two different numbers and only one is on the page.

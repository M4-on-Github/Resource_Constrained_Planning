step 1 Raise the casualty by radio, take the master's account of the grounding, and get her displacement, her drafts now against her drafts before she went on, her tank soundings and whatever she is carrying; confirm the crew is accounted for and that no compartment is flooding.
step 2 Send TUG-007 ahead as the first unit on scene (ETA 1.7 h) because she is quick and handy in shoal water: her job is to mark the site, run a sounding line around the hull and out along the intended escape track, land a tide gauge on the beach or on the casualty's rail, and give me a firm picture of how the vessel is sitting before anything is made fast.
step 3 Put TUG-008 (ETA 1.3 h) to work as the line boat. She has 3 t of bollard pull and is no use on the main pull, but she is the right tool for running messengers and towing wires between the casualty, the beach gear and the heavy tugs, and for ferrying the dive team.
step 4 Bring BG-001 up (ETA 1.2 h) and lay the beach gear astern of the casualty on the reciprocal of her grounding heading: ground anchors well set in holding ground, wire led back to the casualty's after bitts, purchase rigged so the 82 t can be hove in steadily. Ground tackle is the backbone of this refloat because it pulls dead astern and never comes off the lead.
step 5 Land AST-002's dive team (ETA 2.4 h) alongside and have the five divers survey the bottom along the full length of the hull: how far she is set down, whether the keel is bedded or resting on a hard point, whether there is damage to the plating, and whether the escape track aft is clear. Their report decides whether this is a soft sand grounding that will come off on the tide or a hard grounding that needs weight out first.
step 6 Move AST-002's team then to sounding every tank and void alongside the vessel's engineers, so the fuel and hazardous-cargo picture is closed out before any strain is taken.
step 7 Station AST-001 alongside on the deep-water side (ETA 1.4 h) and keep the barge ready to lighter. If the divers find a hard grounding, or if the architect revises the ground reaction upward, discharge cargo or ballast into AST-001 from the areas the architect nominates, working away from the neutral loading point so that the weight removed actually reduces ground reaction rather than merely trimming her. The barge's 1800 t is deck capacity for received cargo and is not pulling force.
step 8 As the tide begins to make, connect the main pull: TUG-002 (ETA 2.2 h) on the centreline stern connection, TUG-001 (ETA 2.2 h) on the port quarter and TUG-005 (ETA 2.7 h) on the starboard quarter, all three leading aft and as near parallel as the ground allows.
step 9 Take the strain in stages from about an hour before high water, heaving BG-001 in on the ground tackle and bringing the three tugs up together under one order from the salvage master, watching the tide gauge so the pull is building as ground reaction falls rather than being wasted on a falling or slack tide.
step 10 At high water, which the architect puts at T+3.3 h, apply the full combined pull and drag her off: BG-001 at 82 t with TUG-002 at 78 t, TUG-005 at 76 t and TUG-001 at 66 t gives 302 t against the 180 t required, and every one of those four is on scene and connected well inside the window. Hold the pull through the top of the tide until she floats free and answers her rudder.
step 11 Hold TUG-003 (ETA 2.4 h), TUG-004 (ETA 2.8 h) and TUG-006 (ETA 3.3 h) at the scene as a connected reserve, made fast but not pulling. They are available inside the window and add a further 205 t if the first application falls short or if a wire parts and a tug has to be replaced mid-pull; I would rather have them standing by than commit the whole fleet and have nothing left.
step 12 The moment she comes free, TUG-002 takes and holds the tow to keep her off the bank; TUG-001 and TUG-005 slack down and stand by to control her head. Re-sound all tanks and bilges at once, because plating that held while she lay on the ground often leaks once she has been dragged across it.
step 13 Recover the ground tackle with TUG-008 and BG-001's crew once the casualty is clear, then tow her under TUG-002 with TUG-007 escorting to the nominated sheltered berth for a class and diver inspection; if she was lightered, AST-001 follows for restowage or discharge ashore.
step 14 Assumptions and limitations: no photograph is available to me, so her list, the extent of any bottom damage and whether she lies bow-to or broadside on the shoal are unknown and must come from TUG-007's first report and AST-002's dive survey; the lead angles in step 8 and the decision on lightering in step 7 both depend on that.
step 15 Limitation on resources not used: BG-002 is excluded on time, not on merit, with an ETA of 17.0 h against a 3.3 h window. TUG-009, TUG-010 and TUG-011, at 5.6 h, 5.6 h and 4.5 h respectively, all arrive after high water and are not counted in any of the figures above.
step 16 Requests: the predicted high-water height and time at the site, so 3.3 h can be tied to a real tidal curve; the seabed type under her, because a rock or coral bottom would push the required pull above 180 t and change the lightering decision; and confirmation from the architect of the neutral loading point before any cargo goes into AST-001.
step 17 Contingency: if she does not come away at the top of this tide, stop rather than overload the gear. Hold her with TUG-002 and BG-001, lighter into AST-001 through the ebb, and make a second attempt on the following high water with the three reserve tugs added to the pull.

step 1 Treat this as a life-safety case first and a righting job second. Establish contact with the vessel or with anyone on the overturned hull, get a muster against the crew list, and determine how many are unaccounted for and which compartments they were last in; progressive flooding means the air pockets inside her are shrinking and that clock runs faster than the 6.6 h righting deadline.
step 2 Mobilise RGT-003 immediately as the first salvage unit on scene (ETA 2.3 h). On arrival her crew establish the safety perimeter, sound around the hull, mark the waterline on the exposed shell with reference marks so the rate of progressive flooding can actually be measured rather than estimated, and tap the hull on a schedule to locate survivors by response.
step 3 Any survivors found inside are brought out before any lifting load goes on the hull. Brief the extraction team to bring people out hand-over-hand along a guided line without kicking, so that each man clear of the opening does not injure the one behind him, and do not cut or vent a compartment that is holding a survivor on an air pocket until the exit is rigged and tended.
step 4 Bring AST-001 up (ETA 4.0 h) and set its 220 m3/h submersible pump to work on the compartments the naval architect nominates, to take out trapped water and slow the progressive flooding. This matters twice over: free surface in a partly flooded hull destroys the righting arm, and trapped water that shifts during the roll will fight the applied moment. The pump supplies no righting moment of its own.
step 5 Have the naval architect run the stability case on the actual soundings and the measured flooding rate and sign off the minimum righting arm and metacentric height the vessel must satisfy at each stage of the roll, together with the sequence in which moment is applied. The decision to roll is taken on those numbers and not on how the hull looks from a boat.
step 6 Order RGT-001 (ETA 3.4 h), RGT-004 (ETA 3.5 h) and RGT-002 (ETA 5.5 h) to proceed at best speed and to rig out on passage, with RGT-002's strand jack set in particular prepared and laid out before arrival, because she lands with only 1.1 h in hand before the deadline and her rig-up time is the critical path of this whole operation.
step 7 On arrival, blow down and position the sponson sets: RGT-003 (3300 t.m) on the low side of the hull first, then RGT-001 (3300 t.m) alongside her, and RGT-004 (1000 t.m) added outboard to extend the lever arm, all pinned to hard points the architect has nominated and not to shell plating that will tear.
step 8 Rig RGT-002's parbuckling strand jacks as the controlled pulling element, with the strands led over the high side of the hull to the pulling foundations, so that the sponson buoyancy and the strand jack pull work on the same roll axis and their moments genuinely add about that axis.
step 9 Right the vessel with all four righting assets working together under one controller: RGT-003 at 3300 t.m, RGT-001 at 3300 t.m, RGT-002 at 3500 t.m and RGT-004 at 1000 t.m give 11100 t.m against the 9200 t.m the architect requires, and all four are on scene inside the 6.6 h window. Roll her in small increments under load control, holding at each increment while the architect confirms the stability case still holds and while AST-001 keeps pace with the water coming out of her.
step 10 Keep the strand jack load on RGT-002 under positive control throughout and never let the roll run away: as she passes through the point where her own residual buoyancy begins to assist, the hull will want to come up faster than the gear is paying out, and an uncontrolled roll will carry her through upright and over to the other side.
step 11 With her upright, hold her in the sponsons from RGT-003, RGT-001 and RGT-004 and do not release the gear. Pump her down hard with AST-001 to re-establish freeboard and positive stability, then have the architect reassess her intact stability and the condition of her shell before anything is slacked.
step 12 Once the architect confirms she is stable and floating on her own lines, slack the strand jacks on RGT-002, recover the gear, and hand her over for a tow to a sheltered berth for inspection.
step 13 Assumptions and limitations: I have no photograph. Whether she is fully inverted or lying on her side is the single most important unknown in this plan, because a fully inverted hull is an upside-down refloating problem with the sponsons set to bring her through 180 degrees, whereas a hull on her beam ends is a side-refloat and the sponson placement in step 7 and the strand jack lead in step 8 both change. RGT-003's first-on-scene report settles it and the rigging plan must be confirmed against it before the gear goes on.
step 14 Second limitation, and the one I would put in front of the client: this works, but it has almost no margin in time. RGT-002 does not arrive until T+5.5 h and the deadline is T+6.6 h. The three sponson sets alone give only 7600 t.m, which is short of the required 9200 t.m, so RGT-002 is not optional and the operation cannot begin its roll until she is rigged. If RGT-002 is delayed by even an hour the window is lost.
step 15 Third limitation: RGT-005 (ETA 67.8 h), RGT-006 (ETA 53.5 h), RGT-007 (ETA 19.0 h) and RGT-008 (ETA 13.7 h) are all far outside the window and none of their capacity can be counted toward this evolution. The 16640 t.m full-fleet figure is not available to us; only 11100 t.m is.
step 16 Requests: expedite RGT-002 by any means available, including partial pre-rigging on the transit, and tell me at once if her ETA slips. I also need a dive team, which this ledger does not include, to inspect the hull and to confirm the sponson and strand jack attachment points before load is applied; and a second dewatering pump, since 220 m3/h is thin cover for a progressive flooding casualty of this size if AST-001 suffers a failure.
step 17 Contingency: if RGT-002 cannot make the window, do not attempt a partial roll on 7600 t.m. Hold the hull where she is with RGT-003, RGT-001 and RGT-004 set as buoyancy to arrest further sinkage, keep AST-001 pumping to hold the flooding, and plan the roll for the next available window with RGT-008 and RGT-007 brought in, accepting that the loss of the air pockets inside her must be assumed in the meantime.

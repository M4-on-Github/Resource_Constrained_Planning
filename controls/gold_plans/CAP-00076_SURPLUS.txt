step 1 Account for the people before anything else. Open communications with the casualty, muster against the crew and any passenger list, and establish how many are missing and where they were last reported. On a large hull with progressive flooding the survivable air pockets are being lost continuously, and that governs the tempo of everything below.
step 2 Push RGT-008 forward as the lead salvage unit, since at ETA 3.9 h she is on scene nearly two hours ahead of anything else. Her tasks on arrival are to take command of the on-water scene, establish the exclusion zone, sound around the hull, set reference marks on the exposed shell so the flooding rate can be measured against time, and run a systematic hull-tapping search for trapped crew.
step 3 Recover any survivors located inside the hull before any crane load goes on. Rig and tend a guided exit line and brief the men to come out hand-over-hand without kicking so each one clear does not injure the next; leave compartments holding air undisturbed until that exit is tended, because venting one to get at it will take the pocket away.
step 4 Have the naval architect build the stability and load case from the actual soundings, the measured flooding rate and the recovered general arrangement, and fix the minimum righting arm and metacentric height the hull must hold at each stage of the roll, the roll axis, and the lift points. The go/no-go for the roll comes off those numbers.
step 5 Order RGT-002 (ETA 5.2 h), RGT-009 (ETA 6.0 h), RGT-010 (ETA 6.1 h) and RGT-005 (ETA 6.7 h) to proceed at best speed and to rig their lifting gear on passage, so that rigging time on scene does not eat the window.
step 6 Moor the crane barges in their working positions on the high side of the hull as they arrive, in that same order, each on its own spread so that the barges are not fouling one another and so that each can hold position independently under load. Pin the lifting gear only to hard points the architect has nominated.
step 7 Have the barge crews pass the parbuckling strands or slings under and around the hull at the architect's nominated stations, with the leads set so that every crane is pulling about the same roll axis; moments only add if the lines of action agree, and on a hull this size a crane rigged off-axis will take load without contributing moment.
step 8 Roll her upright with five crane barges working together under one lift controller: RGT-008, RGT-002, RGT-009, RGT-010 and RGT-005 at 60000 t.m each give 300000 t.m against the 243800 t.m the architect requires, and the last of the five is on scene at T+6.7 h, well inside the 11.9 h deadline. Take the roll in controlled increments, hold at each stage for the architect to confirm the stability case, and log the load on each crane at every step.
step 9 Hold RGT-006 (ETA 6.7 h) and RGT-001 (ETA 6.8 h) at the scene as a rigged reserve, moored and ready but not loaded. Together they are a further 120000 t.m available inside the window, which covers a crane going off-hire mid-roll or the architect revising the required moment upward once he has real soundings. RGT-004 (7.8 h), RGT-007 (8.6 h) and RGT-003 (8.8 h) remain available as a deeper reserve inside the window but are not mobilised to the scene unless called.
step 10 Keep the roll under positive load control throughout. As the hull comes through the angle at which her own residual buoyancy starts to assist, she will try to come up faster than the cranes are paying out; the lift controller must be able to hold or reverse, or she will be rolled through upright and over the other way.
step 11 Station AST-001 (ETA 6.3 h) clear of the crane spreads and use the barge to take off anything that has to come out of the hull during the roll or that is recovered from the water, and to receive cargo or debris the architect wants removed to control weight. Her 1800 t is deck capacity, not righting moment, and nothing in step 8 depends on her.
step 12 With the hull upright, keep her hanging in the cranes and do not release. Dewater and reassess before any gear is slacked, because a large hull that has just been righted with free water in her has very little stability and will roll again if the cranes let go early.
step 13 Once the architect confirms positive stability on her own lines and the shell is sound enough to float, slack the cranes in sequence, recover the rigging, and hand her to a tow for a sheltered berth and a full survey.
step 14 Assumptions and limitations: I am working without the photograph. Whether this hull is fully inverted or lying on her side is the determining unknown, because it changes the rigging in step 7 from a 180-degree upside-down recovery to a side refloat, and it changes where the sling stations go. RGT-008's first-on-scene report settles that and the rigging plan must be confirmed against it before load is applied. I have likewise not assumed any particular damage, list angle or hull opening.
step 15 Limitation on resources: RGT-013 (ETA 50.0 h), RGT-012 (ETA 83.9 h) and RGT-011 (ETA 90.9 h) are days outside the 11.9 h window and none of their capacity is counted anywhere in this plan. The 780000 t.m full-fleet figure is not available to this operation; the 600000 t.m on scene inside the window is, and I have committed 300000 t.m of it with a further 120000 t.m held rigged in reserve.
step 16 Requests: a dive team and an ROV, neither of which appears on this ledger, to survey the hull, confirm the sling and lift-point positions under water, and search the submerged side for trapped crew. Also dewatering pumps, which this ledger does not contain either; on a progressive-flooding casualty of this size I have no means of taking water out of her, and step 12 depends on having some. And confirmation from the architect of the roll axis and the required moment on real soundings, since 243800 t.m is a pre-survey figure.
step 17 A note on the ledger itself for the record: thirteen crane barges of identical 60000 t.m rating, ten of them reachable within twelve hours, is a concentration of heavy lift that does not exist in any single region in practice. I have planned with what is listed as instructed, but the client should be aware that the real-world version of this job is limited by heavy lift availability and would be measured in weeks.

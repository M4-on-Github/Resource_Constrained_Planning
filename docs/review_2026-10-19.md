## 2. Comparison with SOTA

**Already known (the project replicates these):**
- **LLMs rarely flag unsolvable planning instances.** o1-preview flags 27 % of unsolvable Blocksworld instances and 16 % in Mystery Blocksworld, and calls 11.5 % of solvable ones impossible [1].
- **Solvability is the main error source** in tool planning [2], and LMMs fail unsolvable-problem detection [3]. Infeasible requests get confabulated answers [4]. Scale does little for abstention [5].
- **Constraint violation is the norm** in real-world LLM planning [6, 7].
- **The SOTA for feasibility is an external sound solver,** not model judgement. Hao et al. report 93.9 % on TravelPlanner and identify unsatisfiable cores [8]; see also LLM-Modulo [9]. Self-critique degrades planning [10, 11].
- **"Capability present but not surfaced" is established:**
  - in MLLMs, explicit prompting recovers issues models otherwise suppress "in favor of user compliance" [12];
  - embodied abstention rises from 16.5 % to 93.6 % with defensive prompting [13];
  - as a knowing-doing gap in decision-making [14] and tool use [15];
  - as generator-validator inconsistency [16];
  - as hidden representations [17].

**Novel here (no matching work found by five literature searches; not a systematic review):**
- A VLM, an image, and a resource/deadline ledger with **computed ground-truth satisfiability** across **four nested, graded arms**. Prior infeasibility benchmarks are binary and text- or GUI-based [1, 2, 4].
- A **behavioural, per-cell, same-input knowing-vs-doing contrast** in plan generation, with a pre-registered margin. [14, 15, 17] measure gaps through probes of representations or in bandit and tool-call settings, not in free-form plans against a computed truth.
- **Pre-registered equivalence framing** (P4′) rather than a leaderboard number, with deviations logged.
- **Escalation is almost unresponsive to infeasibility:** labelled 0–6 % against the probe's 100 %. This is more extreme than [1]'s 16–27 %, but it is one 8B model.

## 3. Gaps

**Methodological, against current practice:**
- **Single or few prompts.** Multi-prompt evaluation is now expected [18, 19]. G spans +0.24 to +0.82 across two wordings, and only the plan prompt and two probe wordings exist.
- **YES/NO probe with a known "no" lean.** LLMs lean toward "no" [20] and judge more negatively in binary formats [21]; here 128/220 satisfiable cells answer NO to both wordings (`posthoc.txt:17`). A numeric or non-binary probe would remove this.
- **Model-only labels.** Labellers A and B are instances of one model, and LLM errors correlate [22]. Validating against humans is the stated norm [23]. The §10.3 blind human read (`plan.md:1679-1700`) was never done, and every permitted intervention requires it (`plan.md:1652`, `:1695`).
- **Greedy single draw against the card's sampling preset.** The card sets greedy = false with T = 0.7, top_p 0.8, top_k 20 and presence_penalty 1.5 [24]. Greedy loops are 11–33 % and fall with the arm (`rep_blind/report.txt:26-29`), consistent with self-reinforcing repetition [25]. Reproduction is byte-identical on this stack, so numerical nondeterminism [26] is not the issue, but decoding dependence is unmeasured.
- **Confound and covariate.** Ledger size co-varies with arm, and its covariate was never reported (see [new] 2). Irrelevant or long context degrades reasoning [27].
- **One model.** The external-validity gap is acknowledged (`plan.md:1852`).

**Open questions the project can answer cheaply:**
- (a) Does a VLM act on a feasibility fact when it is *stated*? This is the "doing" step that LLM-Modulo pipelines assume [8, 9] and that is rarely measured with infeasible instances.
- (b) Is "knowing" reading a printed aggregate, or aggregating? No-totals is a planned but unrun variant (`plan_v013.md:33`).
- (c) A graded dose-response of abstention, SCARCE vs INFEASIBLE, which binary benchmarks cannot show [1–4].
- (d) Image vs stated state: sunken 7/33 correct, and state branching drops from 66/440 to 5/440 plans when the state is stated (`docs/deviations.md:170`). This bears on text dominance in VLMs [28].

## 4. Recommended next steps (ranked)

**Verdict: Track E is not next.** Its own preconditions are unmet:
- Track M's per-arm rates are not frozen with corrected ESCALATE (`plan.md:1824-1825`, `docs/deviations.md:641`).
- The §10.3 human read required for any permitted intervention does not exist.
- No dev/test split has been declared (`plan.md:1826-1831`).

v0.13 also leaves the *form* of Track E open:
- If the model does not act even when told, assess-then-plan with its own verdict cannot work.
- If its verdict depends on wording, a self-verdict will over-refuse. The `can` wording false-alarms on about 70 % of satisfiable cells.

**Risks:** A single human rater; this is a known limitation (`plan.md:1687`, `:1716`).

### R2. v0.13b: diagnostic on the dev split, pre-registered, no tuning (about 2 probe/plan job-sets)

**What,** on about 40 dev images:
- (a) **Told-verdict plan condition.** The scenario block gains one computed fact line, for example "On-scene capability by the deadline is X t against Y t required: short by Z t", or the matching satisfiable line. This is the solver-in-the-loop ceiling for "doing" [8, 9].
- (b) **No-totals probe.** Remove the totals line and keep both wordings.
- (c) **Two or three more probe wordings,** including a non-binary one: "state the on-scene total by the deadline and the requirement, then FEASIBLE or INFEASIBLE" [18–21].
- (d) Optionally, the named sampling extension (`plan.md:1806`): k = 5 at the card preset on SUFFICIENT and INFEASIBLE.

**Why before Track E:**
- (a) separates "cannot act" from "does not surface". In [12], surfacing was the bottleneck; in [15], the cognition-to-action step was.
- (b) and (c) settle whether K is reading or reasoning, and which verdict wording Track E could trust.

**Expected outcome and what it changes:**
- **E_told high (for example ≥ 0.5 on unsatisfiable cells, over-refusal low on satisfiable).** Acting is achievable, and Track E should be "verdict first, then plan". If (b) and (c) show the self-verdict is fragile, the verdict should be computed rather than self-assessed.
- **E_told low.** The plan genre suppresses escalation even when the fact is stated. Assess-then-plan will not fix it, and the lever is structural, for example a mandatory feasibility field (permitted class, `plan.md:1651`), or another model.
- **K collapses without totals.** v0.13's claim narrows to "reads the printed total when asked in its frame".

**Cost:**
- Probe variants: one probe job (≤ 4 h limit, 64 tokens).
- Told-verdict: 160 plans plus one extraction job.
- Sampling: about 400 plans.
- Labels: escalate labels on the told-verdict plans (A/B plus the R1 human subset).

**Risks:**
- Parroting: the plan may echo "short" without changing commitments. Score OVERCOMMIT and V2a/V3 beside ESCALATE.
- The dev split is used here, so it must be declared first (R1 i).

### R3. Track E (v0.14), shaped by R2

**What:** The arm-conditional objective (`plan.md:1832-1835`), test complement only.
- Assess-then-plan with the verdict source chosen by R2: a self-verdict in the robust wording, or a computed verdict.
- Report correct refusal and over-refusal together (P6; cf. 11.5 % false "impossible" in [1]).

**Why third:** It is the pre-registered lever after GAP (`plan_v013.md:111-113`), and the literature supports it [12, 13]. But its form is undetermined until R2.

**Expected outcome:** A large gain on unsatisfiable arms is plausible [13]. The open question is the over-refusal cost.

**Cost:** About one full v0.10 blind condition on the test complement (4 plan jobs plus extraction), plus labels and the §10.3 re-read.

**Risks:**
- Test n is about 70, so the MDE is larger than v0.10's 15–20 pp (`plan.md:1860`).
- Prompt-level abstention gains are brittle [5, 18].

### R4. Second model and replicates (after R3, or alongside it if GPU time is free)

**What:**
- The v0.10 blind run plus the v0.13 probe on one other VLM, either a larger same-family model or another family.
- k = 5 sampling replicates if they were not run in R2.

**Why:** This is the only route to generality claims. The gap could be model-specific; scale helps abstention little [5].

**Cost:** 8 plan jobs, extraction and one probe job per model.

**Risks:** A different prompt format or chat template may be needed, and template differences are themselves a wording effect.

**Not recommended now:**
- More plan-wording guards. D10 and D12 failed, and [5, 13] say prompts move abstention without fixing it.
- A symbolic baseline as a *score*. It is perfect by construction (`LEDGER-SATISFIABLE` is computed). Its value is as R2's told-verdict input.
- Fine-tuning. It is banned (`plan.md:1654`), and reasoning fine-tuning lowers abstention [5].
- Distractor padding. It is on the README "Not doing" list (`README.md:375-377`). Revisit it in a corpus v2 if [new] 2 holds up.
